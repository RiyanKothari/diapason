# Voiceover for the 60-second film (film muted)

Film: `runs/video/diapason-film-60s.mp4`, with its own sound muted. You narrate all 60 seconds. Each line is sized for
a relaxed pace (about 2.5 words a second); go by what appears on screen rather than the clock.

| Time | On screen | You say |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00 – 0:05** | Title: "Before your voice agent talks to a million Indians…", six callers | "Before your AI voice agent talks to a million Indians, it has to survive these callers." |
| **0:05 – 0:13** | "Agent v1 takes a call": the agent's first line appears | "This is a real test call. My agent opens by telling the borrower his EMI is overdue, and threatens legal action." |
| **0:13 – 0:19** | Ramesh replies; red fail tags light up | "It hasn't even checked who picked up. Diapason flags it the moment it's said." |
| **0:19 – 0:31** | The agent threatens again; the call is failed | "And that's on purpose. I planted seven bugs like this in the agent, to see if Diapason would catch them." |
| **0:31 – 0:41** | "The same agent, behind Diapason Guard" | "Now the same agent, behind Diapason Guard. Every sentence is checked in code before it's spoken. The threat is cut, and the amount waits until he's verified." |
| **0:41 – 0:50** | "Fix, re-test, prove it": the v1 to v4 grid | "Each new version is re-tested on the same calls. All seven bugs were caught, and the final version removes all six text bugs." |
| **0:50 – 0:55** | "After the call: QA on every call" | "After the call, every call is scored, with the agent's own words as proof." |
| **0:55 – 1:00** | "Diapason" with Prisma, Timbre and Evon | "This is Diapason. Built on Gnani's Prisma, Timbre and Evon." |

## Tips

- Speak a little slower than feels natural; smile on the first line.
- Short pauses between scenes are fine. If a line runs long, drop its last few words rather than talking into the next
  scene.

## Recording it

**Snipping Tool, one take:** open the film in Media Player full screen at 0:00; press **Win + Shift + R**, select the
whole screen, turn the **microphone on** and the **system audio off**; click Start, press Space to play, read along,
then Stop and Save.

**Or in Clipchamp:** drag the film onto the timeline, select it, set its volume to 0 (Audio in the right panel), then
record your voice and line it up with the scenes.

Play it back once: your voice clear, no film sound, each line inside its scene.
